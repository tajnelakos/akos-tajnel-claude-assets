# Openers

How the first two lines of a cold message earn the third. These patterns come from a set of outreach emails and LinkedIn messages Akos wrote to banks, insurers and data partners in a previous role. Their product messaging has aged, but their openings still work: each one reaches the point fast and shows the message was written for this one person.

The account names below are placeholders. Every opener still has to rest on a real detail from the account brief ([`skill.md`](./skill.md), rule 7).

## What the good openers have in common

- **The first line is about them:** their exact role and their company, in their own words.
- **One clause about you, in their field's terms.** It isn't a company description.
- **The reason for writing comes before the product,** and that reason is something true about them right now.
- **They end on a question they can answer in one line,** including "not me, try X".

## Patterns

### 1. Name their seat

Open with their exact role and company, worded the way their title reads. Avoid "your company" and avoid flattery.

> I noticed you oversee collateral management at [Bank].
>
> I see that you head up mortgage compliance at [Building society].

This works because it proves you looked, in nine words. The next line has to explain why that role matters to you, so name only a seat you can connect to the trigger.

### 2. Say how you found them, when it's true

An article, a post, a conference sponsor list, a page on their website. Saying where you found them turns a cold message into a reply to something they did.

> I read your comments on rate-switch retention in [Publication] last week.
>
> I saw that [Bank] is also sponsoring [Event] next month.

Never invent the source. If the brief doesn't say where the detail came from, leave the source out.

### 3. Introduce yourself in their field's terms

Describe your own role in one clause, in the reader's vocabulary instead of your company's.

> I work on explainable customer-targeting data for mortgage lenders.

To a compliance head, that clause is about evidence and audit. To a lending head, it's about which customers to call. Use the same person and the same product, but describe them differently for each reader (see [`../../knowledge-base/personas.md`](../../knowledge-base/personas.md)).

### 4. Lead with what their peers are doing about a rule

When the trigger is regulatory, say what other lenders are doing about it, then ask whether it's on their agenda. This makes the regulation the subject, not your product.

> Since [Regulator]'s guidance on [topic], several lenders have asked us how to show why each customer was targeted. Is that on the agenda at [Bank] yet?

Only say "several lenders" if it's true and the brief or `positioning.md` supports it.

### 5. Name a gap you keep seeing

Say what's usually missing, and what needs it, as your own point of view. One sentence, specific to their area.

> What I keep seeing is that the reason behind each targeting decision gets lost between the model and the advisor, while audit is starting to ask for exactly that.

This is the strongest opener when the trigger is a team change or a tooling migration, because the gap is usually what the new hire or the migration exists to fix.

### 6. Ask a real question about their setup (LinkedIn)

Ask a short, direct question about how they do something today. It needs a real answer, so it isn't a rhetorical "Tired of…?" opener (those are banned by the [`branding-guideline`](../../knowledge-base/branding-guideline/skill.md)).

> [Name], how do your advisors decide who to call first each month at [Bank]?

### 7. Mention one connection they'll recognise

Add one connection only if it's real: a sister company, a partner or a mutual client in their group. Use it as a by-the-way line, not as a list of logos.

> By the way, we already work with [Group]'s lending arm in [Country].

### 8. Show care in small ways

When writing in their language isn't your strength, say so in one line and offer to switch. It's honest, and it reads as respect.

> I'm writing in French to make the first read easier, but my French is limited. I'm happy to continue in English if that suits you.

## Asks that pair well with these openers

- **Offer proof on their own data.** For example: "Send me a sample of customer records and I'll show you which moments come up, and why."
- **Make it a yes/no question about relevance, with a referral route out:** "Is this on your plate? If not, who on your team owns it?" A referral is a good outcome for a cold first message.
- **Ask whether the topic matters to them,** for example: "Could this matter for [Bank] this year?" Don't ask about the product.

## What to leave behind from the old messages

The openings in that set were good, but their bodies drifted into the habits listed below. Don't carry them over.

- "I hope this message finds you well" and "Forgive me if you've come across us before." Both delay the first real line.
- "Synergy," "in a nutshell, we specialise in a suite of … modules," "industry-leading," "most comprehensive."
- Long lists of data points or features, and bullet lists of products. Name one thing, the one that answers their trigger.
- Client-logo lists in a first cold message. Use one recognisable connection (pattern 7) or none.
- Benefit-as-subject-line ("Boost your marketing ROI with…"). The subject should name their situation.
- Asking for a demo or a 30-minute call in the first message.
