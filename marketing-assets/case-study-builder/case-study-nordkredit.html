Case study: Bank / Lender, Nordics

Nordkredit replaces a “score only” targeting tool ahead of a board audit

A new Head of Credit Risk needed to show the board why each customer was targeted. Seekridge showed the named data points behind every moment, live, on a pseudonymized sample of the bank’s own customers.

01

Challenge

Nordkredit's newly hired Head of Credit Risk inherited an internal audit finding on her first day: a refinancing campaign had been run off a customer score from the bank's existing data provider, a score and a generic methodology document, not the specific data points that put an individual customer on the list. When an auditor or the internal risk committee asked “why this customer,” the only honest answer was “the model said so”. Not something the team wanted to repeat at an upcoming board audit.

02

Solution

Rather than a prepared demo, Seekridge ran moments live on a pseudonymized sample of Nordkredit's own customers, at the prospect's request, showing the actual property, market and CRM data points behind each moment and their weighting. Nordkredit had already used the same standard (push past the first demo, ask for a real breakdown) to evaluate and rule out another vendor earlier in its process.

03

Results

  • Closed in 65 days, inside Nordkredit's own Tier 1 sales-cycle range.
  • Logged decision driver: “Explainability / evidence trail”.
  • Logged product feedback: “Standout: customer-level evidence trail”.
  • Won at effective price parity: Seekridge's ACV (€56,924) closely tracked the competitor's quoted price (€57,313).
Customer quote

No public-use quote yet. Lena Virtanen's discovery-call comments about the “the model said so” problem are exactly the language a testimonial should use, but they were said on a sales call, not given as a public statement. Using them needs her explicit sign-off first.

Positioning tie-in

“Explainable by design” from the message house: the proof point this account decided on, named data points and weighting per customer rather than a score, is exactly this pillar.

What's not confirmed

Whether the implementation was completed before the board audit. The CRM close date (2026-09-08) falls around that window, but “deal closed” isn't “audit passed”. This case study does not claim it.
