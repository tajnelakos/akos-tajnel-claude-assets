# Product Launch Process

## The problem

Most product launches are run as a list of assets: a deck, a web page, a press release, a LinkedIn post. Each one gets made, and the launch still stalls. Sales can't demo the product in half the markets, CSMs hear about it from their customers, every asset words the product differently, and nobody agreed beforehand what "worked" would mean, so the feedback afterwards is anecdote.

## The approach

One process that runs from shaping the product to measuring it, in five stages. Each step produces something the next step depends on:

- **One messaging master** feeds every asset, so the website, deck, carousel and press release say the same thing.
- **Demos are rated READY, PARTIAL or NOT READY per market and segment**, so sales only shows and claims what actually works on local data.
- **Launch KPIs are set before launch**, so feedback is measured against a target, not collected after the fact.

## The process

### 1. Define

- **Shape the product:** critique features with product and ground them in real use cases for retail banks.
- **Write the messaging master:** one source document sets the tagline, core premise, stage headlines, outcomes and the main illustration that every asset reuses. Built from [`positioning.md`](../../knowledge-base/positioning.md).
- **Name and position:** lock solution and feature names and apply the copy rules in [`writing.md`](../../knowledge-base/design-system/writing.md).
- **Package and price:** set up the Base, Ridge and Summit tiers, market price lists and the CRM, plus an upsell path for existing customers (which upgrade or add-on the launch creates for them).

### 2. Prove

- **Early product pilot:** test the product with 1–2 existing customers before launch.
- **Set launch KPIs:** agree targets with product and sales (for example adoption, demo requests and pipeline), so feedback is measured against them.
- **Build and check market demos:** build a demo for each market and segment with local data, language and relevant moments, then rate each one as READY, PARTIAL or NOT READY, so we know what sales can show and claim before they use it.

### 3. Enable

- **Enable sales:** quick reference doc, enablement deck ([`sales-deck-builder`](../../sales-tools/sales-deck-builder)), demo playbook, market training and updated [`battlecards`](../../sales-tools/battlecard).
- **Enable CSMs and support:** what changes for existing customers, a product FAQ and how to handle first questions and issues.

### 4. Launch

- **Update the website:** segment and solution pages with 30-second videos ([`video-design-system`](../../knowledge-base/video-design-system)), mocked up and then built in Framer.
- **Press release and partner co-marketing:** external announcement of the launch, plus joint announcements and content with partners.
- **Launch to users:** in-app notifications (Appcues), email ([`persona-email-sequence`](../persona-email-sequence)), blog post ([`blog-post-builder`](../blog-post-builder)) and LinkedIn carousel ([`linkedin-carousels`](../../knowledge-base/linkedin-carousels)).
- **Events:** event-specific video, flyer ([`flyer-design-system`](../../knowledge-base/flyer-design-system)), roll-up and a refreshed stand panel.

### 5. Learn: feedback, measured against the launch KPIs

**Early signs of traction (90 days)**

- demo bug checks and demo improvements
- feature adoption and Appcues engagement
- campaign performance: email, LinkedIn, webpage traffic ([example analysis](../persona-email-sequence/campaign-performance-analysis.html))

**Business results**

- sales feedback and objections that come up on calls ([`sales-call-analysis`](../../knowledge-base/sales-call-analysis))
- win/loss rates ([`win-loss-analysis`](../../knowledge-base/win-loss-analysis))
- website demo requests and pipeline influenced
- customer and CSM feedback on the quality of the product's outcomes

What comes back feeds the next round of shaping the product.

## Decisions worth knowing

- **Timing is left out on purpose.** How long each stage takes depends on the product: a new segment launch and a feature release don't share a calendar. The order of the steps is what stays fixed.
- **Demo readiness is checked after the demo is built, not before.** You only know whether a market can be demoed once you've tried it on that market's data.
- **The pilot is a test, not a source of quotes.** Its job is to find out whether the product, use cases and messaging hold up with a real customer before launch.
