# Product Launch Process

## Purpose

A launch fails when assets are made before the basics are settled: what the product is called, what it can be shown doing in each market, and what result counts as success. This skill builds the plan in that order, so every asset reuses one message and every claim matches a demo that works.

## Instructions

1. **Fill in the launch brief.** Use [`launch-brief-template.md`](./launch-brief-template.md), question by question. Three answers can't be guessed and must come from the user: what changes, the segments and the markets — ask if any is missing, since a launch "for everyone" can't be demoed or measured. Fill the rest from the knowledge base and write **open** where an answer is still missing. Pick the **launch size** (Note, Spotlight, Launch) and take the kit from section 7, then adjust it with a reason: an improvement doesn't get a press release, and a new product doesn't stop at a release note.
2. **Show the brief before planning.** One person decides the brief is ready (usually the product lead). Show it, mark the decision as open, and don't present the plan as final until the user confirms it. Everything after this step reuses the brief's names, order and kit.
3. **Define.** Draft the messaging master from [`positioning.md`](../../knowledge-base/positioning.md): tagline, core premise, stage headlines, outcomes and one main illustration. Propose names that follow [`writing.md`](../../knowledge-base/design-system/writing.md) (literal feature names, sentence case, "moments" throughout). Say which tiers (Base, Ridge, Summit) the launch sits in and what upsell path it creates for existing customers.
4. **Prove.** Name 1–2 existing customers as pilot candidates and what the pilot should test. Propose launch KPIs with a target for each, split into early signs of traction (90 days) and business results. List every market × segment demo needed, and leave each one's rating as "to check": READY, PARTIAL or NOT READY is only set once the demo has been built and tried on local data.
5. **Enable.** List the sales assets (quick reference, deck via [`sales-deck-builder`](../../sales-tools/sales-deck-builder), demo playbook, market training, [`battlecard`](../../sales-tools/battlecard) updates) and the CSM and support assets (what changes for existing customers, FAQ, first-issue handling).
6. **Launch.** List the assets in the brief's launch kit only — website, press release and partner co-marketing, user launch (in-app, email, blog, LinkedIn carousel), events — each linked to the repo skill or design system that builds it.
7. **Learn.** Map every KPI from step 4 to where it's measured (CRM, Appcues, campaign reports, [`win-loss-analysis`](../../knowledge-base/win-loss-analysis), [`sales-call-analysis`](../../knowledge-base/sales-call-analysis)).
8. **Restrict claims to READY demos.** Any asset copy in the plan may only claim what a READY demo shows. Mark PARTIAL markets with what's missing.

## What to avoid

- Don't plan before the brief exists, or make assets before the messaging master exists. Each step reuses the one before.
- Don't pick channels by habit. The kit follows the launch size in the brief.
- Don't fill in the decision, names or prices yourself. They are the user's to give.
- Don't add dates or durations unless the user gives them. Timing depends on the product.
- Don't invent pilot results, customer quotes or KPI baselines. Leave them as gaps for the user to fill.
- Don't treat a market as READY because the product works there in theory. It's READY when a demo on that market's data has been built and tested.

## Output format

```
## Launch brief: [official name]

1. What changes, for whom: one sentence / who notices first / existing customers
2. Launch size: Note | Spotlight | Launch
3. Who it's for: buyer / segments / markets (best fit first)
4. The story: before / after and the proof number / proof we can show / first objection / against the competition
5. Name and words
6. Commercials and timing: how it's sold / price / rollout / available through
7. Launch kit and decision: kit / who decides [open] / date [open]

## Launch plan: [what is launching]

**Segments:** ...   **Markets:** ...   **Tiers:** ...   **Size:** ...

### 1. Define
Messaging master: tagline / premise / stage headlines / outcomes / illustration
Names: ...
Packaging and upsell path: ...

### 2. Prove
Pilot: [customers, what it tests]
Launch KPIs: | KPI | Target | Early or business result | Where measured |
Demo readiness: | Market | Segment | Rating (to check) | Missing |

### 3. Enable
Sales: ...   CSMs and support: ...

### 4. Launch
Website / Press and partners / Users / Events

### 5. Learn
Feedback sources mapped to KPIs

---
Open gaps for the user: ...
```
