## Why the small-sample caveat survives into the public post

After the September outdated check, `competitive-landscape.md` and `positioning.md` both say the "0-for-8" and "72.7%" figures are small-sample findings, not settled ones. A caption is exactly the kind of copy that strips caveats for a punchier hook. This draft keeps "small samples, worth watching" in the proof block and gives the raw counts instead of percentages, because publishing the finding without the caveat would itself be an overclaim.

## What changed from a typical post

The first draft of this post ended on "Is the boring incumbent usually the bigger threat? Drop a note below" and four hashtags. Both break the caption rules: the question is engagement bait standing in for a CTA, and Seekridge captions use no hashtags. The final version ends on one plain-verb CTA with one tagged link.
