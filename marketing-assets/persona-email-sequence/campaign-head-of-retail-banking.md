# Deep-Dive Campaign — Head of Retail Banking

The [`example.md`](./example.md) quick-mode sequence for this persona, extended into a full 5-email, A/B-tested campaign — same trigger context, same decision-criteria backbone, run for real against a 240-contact segment. Results: [`campaign-metrics.csv`](./campaign-metrics.csv) (raw sends) and [`campaign-performance-analysis.html`](./campaign-performance-analysis.html) (what the data actually says). See [`personas.md`](../../knowledge-base/personas.md#head-of-retail-banking-buyer-consumer-loans-cards-and-insurance) for the persona this is built from.

**Trigger:** downloaded the "Products per customer: what your transaction data already knows" guide.

**A/B design note:** every email tests two subject-line framings against the same body copy — Variant A states the point directly; Variant B names the persona's pain as a question, in their own words. Body copy is held constant across variants on purpose — varying both at once would make it impossible to attribute a performance difference to either one. **Variant B won on every tracked metric for this persona** (see the analysis): a buyer who has watched customers finance and insure elsewhere responded to that exact situation named as a question.

---

### Email 1 — Day 0

**Variant A:** "Where your customers buy what you could sell them"
**Variant B:** "Who insured the car you just financed?"

You downloaded our guide on products per customer, so you've probably seen the pattern in your own data: a car loan with you, the insurance somewhere else the same week; a renovation financed by another lender; a card that hasn't been upgraded in years.

If you'd like to see how many of those moments sit in a sample of your own customers right now, it takes about 15 minutes, on your data, not a demo.

**Decision criterion advanced:** evidence on their own customers (ranked #1) — moves from the guide's general argument to a concrete look at their own base.

---

### Email 2 — Day 4

**Variant A:** "How we check a moment before you trust it"
**Variant B:** "Would your advisors believe this list?"

The question that decides whether any opportunity list turns into business: will an advisor believe it enough to pick up the phone?

Every Seekridge moment shows the data points behind it — a premium leaving the account each month, a car purchase coming up, an energy rating and the equity to fix it — and how much each counted. Before go-live, we backtest the moments on your own past customers, so you see which ones would have converted.

**Decision criterion advanced:** advisor adoption (ranked #2), through the evidence that makes a moment believable.

---

### Email 3 — Day 9

**Variant A:** "What changes for advisors, and what doesn't"
**Variant B:** "Will anyone open it after month one?"

You've probably bought an advisor tool that nobody used after the first month. So: moments arrive as tasks in the CRM your advisors already use, a short list each week, each with a suggested next step. No new dashboard to remember.

Happy to walk through what that looks like for your setup — no pressure if the timing isn't right.

**Decision criterion advanced:** fit with how each product is sold, and adoption — it addresses adoption risk before any rollout talk, which `personas.md` lists as a trust breaker when skipped.

---

### Email 4 — Day 15

**Variant A:** "How a regional bank raised products per customer"
**Variant B:** "What a peer bank's advisors did differently"

A regional bank on our platform started with car loans and home insurance in two branches. Advisors called customers with a car purchase coming up, or a premium going to another insurer, with the reason in front of them. Six months later all fourteen branches were live, rolled out on the pilot's own numbers.

That's a peer you can talk to directly, not a case study with the names filed off — happy to connect you.

**Decision criterion advanced:** evidence, through a peer reference with a specific outcome — this persona's trust triggers name exactly that.

---

### Email 5 — Day 22

**Variant A:** "Worth 15 minutes on your own customers?"
**Variant B:** "An offer other vendors won't make you"

This is the last email in this sequence — a sixth follow-up on the same topic stops being useful and starts being noise.

If it helps, we'll run moments live on a sample of customers you choose, and walk through the evidence on each one together. If the timing genuinely isn't right, that's a fine answer too.

**Decision criterion advanced:** closes on the strongest trust trigger — a live test on their own customers — rather than an urgency push; `personas.md` lists pushing volume over fit as a trust breaker.
