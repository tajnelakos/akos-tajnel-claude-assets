# Sample output

Deal context and outline for [Seekridge](../../knowledge-base/knowledge-base.md).

---

## Input

> Deal context: First substantive meeting with a regional bank's Head of Retail Banking. They run car-loan, green-loan and card-upgrade campaigns off broad segments, and customers keep insuring elsewhere. A prior vendor (fictional "Propensa") supplied propensity scores that advisors stopped using after a few weeks because nobody could say why a customer was on the list. Risk and the DPO will review whatever they choose.

## Output

```
## Deck Outline — Regional bank, first meeting with Head of Retail Banking
Persona: Head of Retail Banking (buyer: consumer loans, cards and insurance) · Stage: First substantive meeting

1. What your customers buy elsewhere every month
   Purpose: Open on the buyer's growth target (products per customer), not on our product.
   Key point: Car loans financed elsewhere, insurance paid to another insurer, renovations funded by another lender. Each one was a moment in your own data first.

2. Four moments in a retail book
   Purpose: Make it concrete for the products this buyer owns.
   Key point: Buying a car, renovating (green loan), outgrowing the current card, insured elsewhere. One example customer per moment, with the data points that triggered it.

3. Why advisors stopped using the last list
   Purpose: Named-objection slide. The Propensa history is in play in this deal; ignoring it reads as evasive.
   Key point: A score with no reason is a lead nobody calls. Every Seekridge moment carries its reason, so advisors can open the conversation with it.

4. Proof on your own customers
   Purpose: This buyer trusts evidence on their own base over a peer's headline number.
   Key point: Walk through the backtest: how moments ranked in past months performed on the bank's own customers.

5. What changes for advisors
   Purpose: Adoption is this buyer's second criterion.
   Key point: A short weekly list inside the CRM advisors already use, with a suggested next step. No new dashboard.

6. Approved once, not campaign by campaign
   Purpose: Answer the review question before the buyer has to carry it alone.
   Key point: A prepared pack for risk, model governance and the DPO: the data used, how each moment is explained, the audit log. Offer a joint session with the reviewers.

7. Next step: a live test
   Purpose: Specific, action-based next step rather than a generic close.
   Key point: Run moments on a sample of customers their team selects, scheduled before the meeting ends.
```

Note what's absent: no "About Us" slide (this buyer needs proof, not company background) and no generic feature tour. Every slide ties to the growth target, the advisor-adoption objection already in play, or the review the buyer will have to get through.
