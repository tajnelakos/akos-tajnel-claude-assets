# Sample output

Deal context and outline for [Seekridge](../../knowledge-base/knowledge-base.md).

---

## Input

> Deal context: First substantive meeting with a regional savings bank's Head of Deposits & Savings. Deposits are drifting to digital banks in small monthly transfers; the bank's churn model flags customers once the balance has already fallen, and last year's answer was to raise the savings rate for everyone. A prior vendor (fictional "NexaScore") was tried and dropped because nobody could say why a customer was on the list. Risk and the data-protection officer will review whatever is chosen. Product: Seekridge Outflow Watch.

## Output

```
## Deck Outline — Regional savings bank, first meeting with Head of Deposits & Savings
Persona: Head of Deposits & Savings (buyer, Outflow Watch) · Stage: First substantive meeting

1. It's never one big transfer
   Purpose: Open on the buyer's own pain (money leaving in small monthly amounts, noticed when the balance is gone), not on our product.
   Key point: EUR 300 a month to a provider paying a point more looks like nothing, until it's a year of it across thousands of customers.

2. Why raising the rate for everyone is the expensive answer
   Purpose: Name the pricing trap this persona already resents ("matching every rate is ruinous").
   Key point: The cheaper move is to know who is leaving, and call them first with the right offer.

3. One customer, the moment their money started to leave
   Purpose: Make it concrete with the evidence trail: the transaction, customer and market data behind one moment, and their weighting.
   Key point: A new EUR 400 monthly transfer out, income up 16%, other banks paying 1.1 points more — and the date it started.

4. How early, measured on your own data
   Purpose: Pre-empt "we already have a churn model" with the one number that matters to this buyer.
   Key point: A backtest on last year's outflows shows how many weeks before the balance fell each customer would have been flagged.

5. Who acts on it
   Purpose: Show the moment reaching the banker or the savings team, not staying at head office.
   Key point: Each moment lands in the CRM queue of whoever owns the customer, with the reason attached.

6. Approved once, not campaign by campaign
   Purpose: Remove the review burden from the buyer, given that risk and the DPO will look at it, and given the NexaScore history.
   Key point: A ready review pack — method note, a real evidence trail, data-processing terms, and the rule that moments only describe what has already happened — for the buyer to forward.

7. Next step: a backtest on your own outflows
   Purpose: Specific, action-based next step rather than a generic close.
   Key point: Agree the sample (customers who moved money out last year) and the date the backtest results come back, before the meeting ends.
```

Note what's absent: no "About Us" slide (this buyer needs proof on their own data, not company background), no rate-matching hook (a stated trust breaker for this persona) and no risk-led opening — risk and data protection are reviewers here, reached through the pack in slide 6.
