SeekridgevsPocketlens

A customer-facing money-insights widget for banking apps. Rarely in our bank deals, but a real rival at credit unions, where it is cheap, familiar and already installed.

Their pitch, and the quick dismiss

They say

“Money insights your members will love, inside your banking app.”

Quick dismiss

It works in the app, but nothing reaches the banker: buyers tell us most members open it twice and stop. It has no ranked opportunities, no evidence trail, and nothing lands in the CRM.

Pocketlens gives members a spending summary. We give the bankers the reason to call, in the tools they already use.

Where they’re strong, and what to say

  • Already installed and familiar
    Don’t attack it. Ask how often members open it, and whether bankers ever see it.
  • Simple per-user pricing
    Match on clarity, not price. Show the cost per conversation that turns into a product, not per user.

Where they’re weak, and how to use it

  • Members stop opening it
    Offer a pilot measured on banker conversations, the result buyers care about.
  • Built for members, not bankers
    Insights for customers and opportunities for bankers are different jobs; Pocketlens can stay in the app.

Where we’re weaker, and how to handle it

  • Nothing customer-facing
    Don’t compete on app features. Compete on what bankers do with each moment and the reason behind it.

Landmines to lay

  1. “How many members open the insights every week?”Repeat use is the gap buyers already feel.
  2. “When a member opens an insight, does a banker ever see it?”Opens the bank-facing conversation.
  3. “What would make a new tool stick with your bankers this time?”Lets the buyer describe the pilot we should run.

What buyers told us

Don’t say

  • “Pocketlens is a bad product.” Members like parts of it. We win on what bankers do, not on disparaging it.
  • “It’s free anyway.” No longer true: the free tier became a 90-day paid pilot in October 2026.
