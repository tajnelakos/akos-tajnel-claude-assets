Their pitch, and the quick dismiss

They say

“Seller leads that help your agents win more listings.”

Quick dismiss

It works for agents, but adoption is its weak spot: buyers tell us half their agents barely use it. It has no customer-data governance, and it isn’t built for a bank’s lending team.

PropIQ gives agents a list of leads. We give the whole team the reason to call, in the tools they already use.

Where they’re strong, and what to say

  • Already installed and familiar
    Don’t attack it. Ask what share of agents use it every week.
  • Simple per-seat pricing
    Match on clarity, not price. Show the cost per listing won, not per seat.

Where they’re weak, and how to use it

  • Low adoption after the first weeks
    Offer a pilot measured on agent usage, the problem buyers name first.
  • Not built for banks
    For a bank with a brokerage arm, one platform for lending and brokerage is simpler than two vendors.

Where we’re weaker, and how to handle it

  • Fewer brokerage-only features
    Don’t compete feature by feature. Compete on adoption and on the reason behind each lead.

Landmines to lay

  1. “How many of your agents use the tool every week?”Adoption is the gap buyers already feel.
  2. “Does your lending side use a different data tool from your brokerage team?”Opens the one-platform conversation in banks.
  3. “What would make a new tool stick with agents this time?”Lets the buyer describe the pilot we should run.

What buyers told us

Don’t say

  • “PropIQ is a bad product.” Agents like parts of it. We win on adoption, not on disparaging it.
  • “It’s free anyway.” No longer true: the free plan became a 14-day trial in October 2026.
