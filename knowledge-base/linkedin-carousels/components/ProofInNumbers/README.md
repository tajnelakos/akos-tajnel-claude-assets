# ProofInNumbers

One result told honestly: the figure on the cover, what it measures, a chart against its baseline, how it was produced, where it stops, and a close.

**Use it for** a backtest, a pilot result or a production metric. The format exists because positioning is moving from "we have explainability" to proof at scale (see [`../../../positioning.md`](../../../positioning.md)): one well-sourced number beats five unsourced ones. 6 slides.

**You provide**
- One figure, real and approved, or clearly labelled illustrative. 6 characters at most for `figure-hero` ("68%"); longer amounts use `figure`.
- What it measures, in one sentence of plain words ("of flagged customers refinanced within six months").
- The source: data, period and sample size ("Backtest on 18,400 mortgage customers, 2025").
- The baseline it's compared with, and one or two comparison values for the chart.
- The method in three sentences: cut-off date, where the outcomes came from, who reviewed it.
- The limits in one statement ("One lender, one year, one product.").
- A close that offers the same test on the reader's own data.

**Build rules**
- Cover: lockup top-left, then `figure-hero` (paper, never gold), the measure in `body-lead`, the source line in mono, as one block anchored 240px from the bottom. No motif. Arrow at the margin.
- Meaning: `plot-strip` motif along the top edge (the eyebrow moves down to 192px), `title-statement` at top ≈ 340px restating what the figure counts, then the baseline in `body-lead`. The strip's lot is the slide's gold.
- Chart: a paper slide. A `heading` that states the takeaway, then horizontal bars 72px tall, 48px apart: the bar the post is about in `gold`, every other bar in `navy`, labels in Plex Sans, values in mono on the right. A source line under the chart.
- Method: navy, `heading` plus a three-row checklist.
- Limits: `title-statement`, one `body-lead` line, and the `scale-bar` motif under it.
- Close: as every close.

**Don't**
- Don't round up, say "up to", or drop the sample size or the word "illustrative" to make the cover hit harder.
- Don't make the figure gold or outline it. The number is the proof; it stays in the text colour.
- Don't put two hero figures on one slide, or more than one gold bar in a chart.
- Don't skip the Limits slide. It's the slide this reader believes the rest because of.
