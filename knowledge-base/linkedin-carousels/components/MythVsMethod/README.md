# MythVsMethod

Corrects a belief the market repeats: the correction as a cover, the belief in an outlined card, why it fails under scrutiny, what a defensible method looks like, and a close.

**Use it for** thought leadership aimed at a sceptical reader, usually one of the two buyers (Head of Retail Banking, Head of Deposits & Savings) or a Data & CRM Lead — never risk teams directly, who get the review pack through the buyer. It works because the brand's values (*defensible over impressive*, *respect the skeptic*) are the argument. 5 slides.

**You provide**
- The correction as a cover line, stated as a fact, not a question ("Explainability can't be added after the model."). About 8 words.
- The belief, in the words the market uses, as a quotation: up to 15 words. It's a paraphrase of a common position, never a quote from a named competitor or person.
- Why it fails: one statement about the question it can't answer (a regulator's, a risk committee's, a customer's), plus one supporting line.
- The method: three checks, each a full sentence a buyer could put to a vendor.
- A close that turns the method into an action ("Ask any vendor for one moment's evidence.").

**Build rules**
- Cover: lockup top-left, `ridge-lines` motif top-right, title block anchored 240px from the bottom and 160px clear of the right edge, `body-lead` line "A belief worth retiring, in four slides." or similar. Arrow at the margin.
- Belief: an outlined card (2px `paper-35`, 6px corners, no fill) at top ≈ 300px with a mono label ("What the market says") and the belief in `title-statement`, in typographic quotes. No gold on this slide: the belief is never highlighted.
- Scrutiny: `title-statement` with the one word that carries the question in `gold-light` ("regulator", "risk committee").
- Method: a paper slide, so the turn from belief to method is visible as a change of ground. `heading` with up to 3 words underlined in gold, then the checklist.
- Close: as every close: `title-cover`, one line, lockup centred at the bottom.

**Don't**
- Don't name or imply a competitor, and don't put a real person's words in the belief card.
- Don't use "It's not X, it's Y" or "Myth: … Fact: …" labels. The outlined card and the paper ground carry the contrast.
- Don't strike through the belief or mark it with red, a cross or a warning icon. The reader may hold it; respect that.
- Don't add a product screenshot. The method is the point; the product belongs to the close or the caption.
