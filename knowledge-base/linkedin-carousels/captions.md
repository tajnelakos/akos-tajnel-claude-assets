# Writing the LinkedIn caption

The caption carries what the slides leave out: the detail, the method, the logistics and the one link. It follows [**Writing for carousels**](./wording.md), with the differences below.

## Anatomy: four short blocks, 50–110 words

| Block | Job | Patterns that work |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Hook** (1–2 lines) | Stop the scroll with a situation or a fact the reader recognises. It must not repeat the cover word for word | Situation: "Most of your fixed-rate customers will decide where to refinance months before they call you." · Fact with source: "In a backtest on one lender's 2025 book, 68% of the customers we flagged refinanced within six months." · Rule: "The EU AI Act classes credit scoring as high-risk AI." |
| **2. What changes, as a benefit** (2–3 lines) | Name the feature once, then say what the reader can now *do* | "Evidence Trail now opens every signal with a one-line reason, written from the data points behind it, so an advisor can say why before they call." |
| **3. Proof or detail** (1–2 lines) | The figure with its source, the method, or the logistics | "Signals were built on data up to 1 January 2025; outcomes came from the lender's own CRM six months later." · "Stockholm, 12–13 March, stand B14. Talk on 13 March at 11:30." |
| **4. CTA + one link** (1 line) | A plain verb, what they get, then the link | "See a sample signal: [link]" · "Ask for a backtest on your own book: [link]" · "Book a slot at the stand: [link]" · "Read the full brief: [link]" |

## Rules

- **Situation first, product second.** Never open with "We're excited to announce" or "Introducing". Parcelwise and the feature name appear in block 2, not line 1.
- **No rhetorical-question hooks** ("Tired of…?", "Ever wondered…?"). A direct question that names a real situation is allowed, at most one per caption.
- **Talk about the reader's customers.** The strongest lines give the reader something to say to *their* customer or their risk committee.
- **We and our are fine in captions** ("Our method is documented here"), unlike on most slides.
- **Every figure keeps its source and its caveat.** Short-form copy is where a sample size or "illustrative" gets cut for a punchier hook; it never does. If the figure can't carry its source in the caption, it doesn't go in the hook.
- **No hashtags.** Tag people and organisations instead (a speaker, an event organiser), using their LinkedIn handle, and only with their agreement.
- **Emoji: at most one**, at the end of a line, never in the hook and never on slides. Most Parcelwise captions use none.
- **Links:** one per caption, with UTM tags (utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=…&utm_content=…) or a short link. Never in the first comment as a workaround.
- **Short paragraphs** separated by an empty line. Lists (session times, checks) one item per line, no bullets or emoji as bullets.
- **Length:** keep the hook and block 2 inside LinkedIn's first ~210 characters before "…see more", and the whole caption under 1,300 characters.
- **Language:** UK English with contractions; German *Sie*; Dutch *u* for banks and insurers, *je* for brokers; Nordic *du*. Follow each market's typography („…“ in German, «…» in Swiss German and Norwegian, ”…” in Swedish).
- **No exclamation marks**, except one in a warm, local sign-off on an event post ("Bis bald!").

## Pairing caption and slides

- The cover and the hook do different jobs: the cover names the situation in 8 words; the hook adds the fact or the stake. Never paste one into the other.
- Slides show the benefit and the proof. The caption adds the method, the detail and the action.
- The link lives in the caption only, never on a slide.
- If the slides use an illustrative figure, the caption says so in the same words ("illustrative").

## Examples

**Signal explainer** (82 words)

> Most fixed-rate customers decide where to refinance months before they call their bank.
>
> A Parcelwise signal shows which customers are likely to move, and why: the rate end date from your CRM, the equity in the home, and what local lenders are offering. Each data point carries its weight, so an advisor can open the call with the reason.
>
> The weights in this carousel are illustrative.
>
> See a signal built on your own book: [link]

**Proof in numbers** (74 words)

> In a backtest on one lender's 2025 book, 68% of the customers we flagged refinanced within six months. Of those we didn't flag, 9% did.
>
> Signals were built on data up to 1 January 2025; outcomes came from the lender's own CRM six months later, and the method was documented for their model-risk team.
>
> One lender, one year, one product. Ask for the same backtest on your own book: [link]

## Before you post the caption

1. Is the hook a situation or a sourced fact, and different from the cover?
2. Is the feature named once, in block 2, as a benefit?
3. Does every figure keep its source, its sample and "illustrative" where it applies?
4. One link, no hashtags, at most one emoji, no exclamation marks?
5. Does it pass [`positioning-compliance`](../../marketing-assets/positioning-compliance)?
