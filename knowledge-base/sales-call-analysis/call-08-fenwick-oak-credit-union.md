# Call Transcript — Fenwick & Oak Credit Union

**Date:** August 2026 · **Duration:** ~15 minutes · **Call type:** First discovery call
**Rep:** Marcus Webb (Seekridge) · **Prospect:** Priya Chandra, Relationship Manager Lead, Fenwick & Oak Credit Union (credit union, ~50,000 members, ~25 relationship managers)

---

**Marcus:** Priya, thanks for the time. What's prompting you to look at a tool like this now specifically?

**Priya:** We're losing members a little at a time, our bankers think — not closed accounts, just money. Savings drifting to digital banks, a mortgage taken somewhere else by someone whose salary we've had for ten years. And when we do get them in for a conversation, the other bank has already made its offer. It's not a crisis, but it adds up.

**Marcus:** Understood. Before we get into the product — how do your bankers currently decide who to call, and what do they bring into the conversation?

**Priya:** Mostly gut feel — who they remember, plus whatever they can see in the core system, which varies a lot by banker experience level. Our newer bankers especially struggle; they don't have the member history experienced bankers carry in their heads.

**Marcus:** That's actually a good way to frame the value — less "replace what experienced bankers do well" and more "give newer bankers the floor that experienced bankers already have in their head."

**Priya:** That's exactly the gap, yes. Okay, what's this going to cost per banker? I need to be direct about that because seat-based pricing across 25 bankers adds up fast for a credit union our size.

**Marcus:** Fair, and I'd rather give you real numbers than a vague range. It's seat-based, tiered by team size — at 25 bankers you'd likely land in [a specific tier], which I can confirm precisely rather than estimate loosely.

**Priya:** Okay. And realistically, how long does it take a banker to actually get comfortable using this day to day? I don't want to pay for 25 seats and have half the team never actually adopt it.

**Marcus:** That's the right question to ask before the pricing question, honestly. I'll be straightforward: onboarding for someone comfortable with basic software tools is usually fast, same day for the week's moments and the reasons behind them. For bankers less comfortable with new tools generally, it can take longer to build the habit — that's more about individual comfort with new software than the product's complexity, but I don't want to pretend everyone picks it up instantly.

**Priya:** That tracks with my team — I've got a wide range of comfort levels. Is there a lighter onboarding path for the less tech-comfortable half of my team specifically?

**Marcus:** That's a fair ask, and honestly not something I have a fully specific answer for today — most onboarding material assumes a baseline comfort level. Let me check with our customer success team on whether there's a simplified path for less tech-comfortable users, rather than assume there is one.

**Priya:** I'd appreciate that — it's the actual risk in this for me, more than the per-seat cost.

**Marcus:** Understood, I'll treat that as the real open question rather than pricing. Can I follow up by early next week with a real answer on onboarding support specifically for less tech-comfortable users, plus the exact pricing tier for your team size?

**Priya:** Yes, that works. If the onboarding story is solid, pricing at that tier is workable for us.

**Marcus:** Good to know pricing itself isn't the blocker if onboarding is handled well. I'll get back to you with both by early next week.
