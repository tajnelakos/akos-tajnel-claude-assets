# Call Transcript — Harborlight Credit Union

**Date:** August 2026 · **Duration:** ~13 minutes · **Call type:** First discovery call
**Rep:** Marcus Webb (Seekridge) · **Prospect:** Dana Whitfield, Relationship Manager Lead, Harborlight Credit Union (credit union, ~120,000 members, ~60 relationship managers)

---

**Marcus:** Dana, thanks for the time. What made you take this call?

**Dana:** Curiosity more than urgency, I'll be honest. We've had Pocketlens in our member app for about two years. It's not perfect, but it's embedded — members know it, and our bankers send people to it when they ask about their spending.

**Marcus:** Given that, what's the actual motivation to even take a call like this?

**Dana:** A newer banker who came from another bank mentioned your platform and said the moments she got there were noticeably better — the reasons behind each call. That planted a seed, but I want to be upfront — adding another tool across 60 bankers is a real undertaking, not something I'd do for a marginal improvement.

**Marcus:** That's a fair bar to set, and I'd rather be direct about what would and wouldn't justify that disruption. Can I ask what specifically she said was better?

**Dana:** Mostly that it told her which members to call and why — a salary that had gone up, money starting to go to another bank — and that she could walk a member through the reason without it feeling like a sales script.

**Marcus:** That tracks with how we're built — the goal is giving a banker something they can actually talk a member through, not just a name on a list. But I want to be honest with you: is "better reasons to call members" actually a top-3 problem for your team today, or is it a nice-to-have that one banker happened to notice?

**Dana:** Fair question. Honestly, no — adoption and consistency across 60 bankers is my actual top problem, not the depth of any single tool. Half my team barely uses our core system's member view properly as it is.

**Marcus:** Then I don't think a "our moments are more detailed" pitch is the right conversation for you right now, and I'd rather tell you that than manufacture urgency that isn't real. If the real problem is inconsistent adoption of the tools you already have, adding a platform doesn't obviously fix that — it might just add another adoption curve and create a worse problem temporarily.

**Dana:** That's refreshingly candid for a sales call, I'll say that.

**Marcus:** I'd rather have a shorter, honest conversation than a longer one that leads nowhere real. Here's what I'd suggest instead: keep our details on file. If losing members' money to other banks ever becomes an actual blocker — not "one banker noticed something," but a real operational problem — that's the moment this conversation is worth having properly, likely alongside a broader adoption push regardless of which tools you land on.

**Dana:** That's a reasonable way to leave it. I'll keep you in mind if something changes structurally, not just anecdotally.

**Marcus:** Sounds good. No follow-up pressure from my side — genuinely just glad you had a clear-eyed read on your own actual priority.

## Internal notes (rep-logged)

- Low buying moment, and correctly identified as such rather than pursued — the actual trigger ("one banker's anecdote") wasn't a real operational problem, and the rep explicitly said so rather than manufacturing urgency.
- Useful pattern: Pocketlens is embedded on the member side, but it does a different job (customer-facing insights); the real competitive risk at this account is inertia/adoption-curve cost, not a feature comparison — consistent with the "adoption over features" theme also seen in [`call-04-riverton-credit-union.md`](./call-04-riverton-credit-union.md).
- Good practice: rep explicitly told the prospect their stated motivation didn't justify the disruption of adding a tool, rather than pushing forward anyway to protect the call. Likely built more credibility for a genuine future re-engagement than a harder push would have.
- No action items — correctly closed as low-priority nurture, no urgency, no false promises made either direction.
