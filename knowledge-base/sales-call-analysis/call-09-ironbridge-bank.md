# Call Transcript — Ironbridge Bank

**Date:** July 2026 · **Duration:** ~14 minutes · **Call type:** First discovery call
**Rep:** Anna Kowalski (Seekridge) · **Prospect:** Fredrik Nilsson, Head of Model Risk, Ironbridge Bank (regional retail bank)

---

**Anna:** Fredrik, thanks for the time. What's the context behind reaching out now?

**Fredrik:** I'll be honest, the timing is awkward — we're about four months into implementing NexaScore. This call is more due diligence on my part than an active evaluation.

**Anna:** Appreciate you being upfront about that rather than letting me think this is a live deal. Can I ask what's prompting due diligence mid-implementation — is something not going well, or is this just healthy vendor awareness?

**Fredrik:** A bit of both, honestly. Implementation's taken longer than we were told, and the explanation feature — which was a big part of why we picked them — is technically live but our team's found it thinner in practice than the sales demo suggested. It shows which feature categories mattered, but not the actual transactions and market data points, and the moments are noticeably weaker for some kinds of customer.

**Anna:** That's useful and specific — do you know if that's consistent across customer types, or worse for some than others?

**Fredrik:** Worse on sole traders and small-business owners — people who run their business through a personal account — from what my analysts have seen. Salaried customers seem more solid.

**Anna:** Okay. I don't want to pile on a competitor you're already contracted with — that's not a useful use of this call. What would actually be useful to you right now?

**Fredrik:** Candidly, just knowing what my options are if this doesn't improve. I don't want to be back here in eight months having burned a year on a vendor switch that didn't pan out, with no sense of alternatives.

**Anna:** That's reasonable. I'd suggest we don't treat this as a sales conversation today — more like: I'll share what our evidence trail actually looks like for sole traders and small-business owners specifically, since that's your live pain point, and you can judge for yourself whether it's a meaningfully different depth of detail.

**Fredrik:** That would be useful, yes.

**Anna:** *(walks through a business-customer example)* ...this is an outflow moment for a sole trader — the business payments separated from the household spend, a new monthly transfer to another bank, the market data, the CRM fields, and the weighting on each. I'd encourage you to compare this directly against what NexaScore actually produced for a similar customer, not from memory.

**Fredrik:** That is more detailed on the sole-trader side than what we've seen. I'm not going to make any decisions today, to be clear — we're contracted, and switching mid-implementation would be its own mess.

**Anna:** Understood, and I wouldn't push you to. What I'd suggest: keep this on file, and if the sole-trader gap doesn't improve over the next few months, reach back out — I'd rather be a known, low-pressure option than someone who pushed too hard at the wrong moment.

**Fredrik:** I appreciate that approach, honestly. I'll keep this on file and revisit if things don't improve.

**Anna:** Sounds good. No urgency from my side — just wanted you to have real information rather than nothing.
