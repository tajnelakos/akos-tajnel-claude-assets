# Call Transcript — Fenwick & Oak Bank

**Date:** August 2026 · **Duration:** ~15 minutes · **Call type:** First discovery call
**Rep:** Marcus Webb (Seekridge) · **Prospect:** Priya Chandra, Head of Advisory Network, Fenwick & Oak Bank (small private bank, DACH, ~39,000 customers, ~25 advisors)

---

**Marcus:** Priya, thanks for the time. What's prompting you to look at this now specifically?

**Priya:** We're losing a couple of good customers a month, our advisors think, to banks that get to them first. Sometimes people who've banked with us for years quietly move their salary, and we notice when the account goes quiet. It's not a crisis, but it adds up.

**Marcus:** Understood. Before we get into the product, how do your advisors currently decide who to call, and what do they open the conversation with?

**Priya:** Mostly gut feel: who they remember, plus whatever they can see in the account when they think to look, which varies a lot by experience. Our newer advisors especially struggle. They don't have the customer history experienced advisors carry in their heads.

**Marcus:** That's actually a good way to frame the value. Less "replace what experienced advisors do well" and more "give newer advisors the floor that experienced advisors already have in their heads."

**Priya:** That's exactly the gap, yes. Okay, what's this going to cost? I need to be direct about that, because pricing adds up fast for a bank our size.

**Marcus:** Fair, and I'd rather give you real numbers than a vague range. It's priced by the number of customers analysed. At around 39,000 customers you'd likely land in [a specific tier], which I can confirm precisely rather than estimate loosely.

**Priya:** Okay. And realistically, how long does it take an advisor to get comfortable using this day to day? I don't want to pay for it and have half the team never adopt it.

**Marcus:** That's the right question to ask before the pricing question, honestly. I'll be straightforward: for someone comfortable with basic software, onboarding is usually same day for the moment list and the evidence behind each moment. For advisors less comfortable with new tools generally, building the habit can take longer. That's more about individual comfort with new software than the product's complexity, but I don't want to pretend everyone picks it up instantly.

**Priya:** That tracks with my team. I've got a wide range of comfort levels. Is there a lighter onboarding path for the less tech-comfortable half specifically?

**Marcus:** That's a fair ask, and honestly not something I have a fully specific answer for today. Most onboarding material assumes a baseline comfort level. Let me check with our customer success team whether there's a simplified path, rather than assume there is one.

**Priya:** I'd appreciate that. It's the actual risk in this for me, more than the cost.

**Marcus:** Understood, I'll treat that as the real open question rather than pricing. Can I follow up by early next week with a real answer on onboarding for less tech-comfortable advisors, plus the exact pricing tier for your size?

**Priya:** Yes, that works. If the onboarding story is solid, pricing at that tier is workable for us.

**Marcus:** Good to know pricing itself isn't the blocker if onboarding is handled well. I'll get back to you with both by early next week.
