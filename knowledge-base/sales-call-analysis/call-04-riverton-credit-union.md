# Call Transcript — Riverton Credit Union

**Date:** July 2026 · **Duration:** ~16 minutes · **Call type:** First discovery call
**Rep:** Marcus Webb (Seekridge) · **Prospect:** Tomas Reyes, Relationship Manager Lead, Riverton Credit Union (credit union, ~85,000 members, ~40 relationship managers)

---

**Marcus:** Tomas, thanks for hopping on. I saw you downloaded the banker productivity piece — what prompted that?

**Tomas:** We're evaluating whether to give our relationship managers a way to see which members are worth a call this week. Right now they work from a list of members with high balances, or wait on me to guess who might need something, which doesn't scale past a certain team size. And when they do get the meeting, they often can't say why they reached out in the first place.

**Marcus:** Makes sense. Walk me through what a member conversation looks like today without a tool.

**Tomas:** Two problems. First, we hear a member moved their savings or took a mortgage somewhere else after the fact — we had their salary coming in the whole time. Second, when a banker does get a member in the room, the member asks "why are you calling me?", and the banker either fumbles an answer or falls back on a generic product pitch, which loses the conversation. Members stay with the banker who shows up knowing what's actually going on in their finances.

**Marcus:** Got it — so the tool's job is to get your bankers to the right members first, and then give them something real to say in the room, not just produce a report afterward.

**Tomas:** Right. We actually piloted Pocketlens in our app last year — the spending summaries for members.

**Marcus:** What was the read on it?

**Tomas:** Mixed. Some members liked it. Most opened it twice and never came back. And my bankers never saw any of it — it lived in the app, not in their day. Which is my actual worry with any tool — adoption, not features.

**Marcus:** That's the right worry to have, honestly — a lot of tools die in month two, not because the feature set was wrong but because they added friction instead of removing it. Can I ask what your bankers would actually need for this to stick?

**Tomas:** Half of them visit members — farms, small businesses, people's kitchens. They'd need to pull up a member on a phone in the car park before walking in, not go back to a laptop at the branch.

**Marcus:** That's a real gap, and I want to be straight with you rather than oversell — our mobile view today covers the week's moments and the reasons behind each, but it's not yet as polished as I'd want for someone pulling it up in a car park thirty seconds before a meeting. It's on our roadmap, but I'm not going to claim it's solved if it isn't.

**Tomas:** I appreciate you saying that directly instead of dancing around it. How far off is it?

**Marcus:** I don't have an exact date I can commit to on this call — I'd rather follow up with a real answer from our product team than guess. What I can say is the desktop and tablet experience is strong today, and for bankers planning their week rather than checking live on the doorstep, it already solves the core problem you described.

**Tomas:** That's actually most of our use case, if I'm honest — the car-park scenario is maybe 20% of it. The bigger issue is knowing who to call this week, and prep before the meeting — walking in knowing their income went up, or that money has started going to another bank, and why that matters.

**Marcus:** Then that's worth testing directly rather than theorizing about it. Would a small pilot with maybe five bankers make sense — the ones who are already comfortable with tools, not your most skeptical group — before rolling to all 40?

**Tomas:** That's smart, actually. Doing it broadly and having it fail from adoption issues would set us back further than testing it small first.

**Marcus:** I'll follow up on the mobile roadmap question separately so you have a real answer, not a placeholder. For the pilot — do you want to pick the five bankers, or should we suggest a mix of tenure/comfort levels?

**Tomas:** I'll pick them — I know who'd actually give it a fair shot versus who'd look for reasons to go back to old habits.

**Marcus:** Sounds good. I'll send pilot details and follow up on mobile by end of week.

**Tomas:** Appreciate it. Let's see how the five-banker group does before we talk numbers for the full team.

- Buying moment: moderate, contingent on a successful small pilot — not yet a real commercial moment, don't overweight this as "closing."
